Band Gap of Germanium Semiconductor

Four Probe Method · Resistivity Measurement · Energy Gap Estimation

SYSTEM READY — Set temperature and current, then record measurements.

Principle

The four probe method eliminates contact resistance errors by using separate current-injecting and voltage-sensing probes. Resistivity is measured as a function of temperature to extract the band gap energy.

ρ = V/I × 2πs · f(W/S)

Band Gap Theory

For an intrinsic semiconductor, resistivity varies exponentially with temperature. Plotting ln(ρ) vs 1/T gives a straight line whose slope yields the band gap energy Eg.

ln(ρ) = Eg/2k · (1/T) + C

Germanium Data

Germanium is an ideal semiconductor for this experiment with a relatively small band gap, making it easy to observe intrinsic conduction at accessible temperatures (300–500 K).

Eg(Ge) ≈ 0.67 eV at 300 K

Procedure
1
Place the Germanium crystal (n-type or p-type) on the four-probe arrangement and ensure good contact between probes and the sample surface.
2
Connect current source to outer probes (P₁ and P₄) and voltmeter to inner probes (P₂ and P₃). Record probe spacing 's'.
3
Set initial temperature to 300 K using the temperature controller and allow the sample to reach thermal equilibrium.
4
Pass a constant current I through outer probes. Note the voltage V across inner probes from the voltmeter.
5
Calculate resistivity ρ = (V/I) × 2πs × G(W/S), where G(W/S) is the geometric correction factor for sample thickness W.
6
Increase temperature in steps of 10–20 K up to 500 K. Record V at each temperature. Compute ρ and ln(ρ) for each reading.
7
Plot ln(ρ) on Y-axis vs 1/T (×10⁻³ K⁻¹) on X-axis. Draw best-fit straight line through the linear region.
8
Calculate slope m of the graph. Band gap Eg = 2k × m, where k = 8.617 × 10⁻⁵ eV/K (Boltzmann constant).
